# Healthy Growth at Age 4

A one-page resource for parents of preschoolers. It explains what normal growth looks like at age 4 and includes a simple checker that compares two height measurements against the typical yearly pace.

## What's on the page

- **Yearly growth checker.** Enter two heights taken 6 to 24 months apart, in inches or centimeters. The page converts the difference to a yearly rate and compares it with the typical 2 to 3 inches (5 to 7.5 cm) a year for this age. A doorframe-style ruler shows both marks against the usual height range at age 4.
- **Typical heights at age 4.** The 5th, 50th, and 95th percentile values from the CDC growth charts for boys and girls.
- **Everyday habits.** Sleep, active play, nutrition, and checkup guidance drawn from the American Academy of Sleep Medicine, the World Health Organization, the Institute of Medicine, and the American Academy of Pediatrics.
- **When to talk to a pediatrician.** The growth patterns worth raising at a well-child visit.

## Key facts

- Most children between 3 and 6 grow about 2 to 3 inches a year.
- The median height at age 4 is about 40.4 inches for boys and 39.8 inches for girls, with a healthy range of roughly 37 to 43 inches.
- Genetics sets most of a child's height range. Daily habits help a child reach it.
- Steady growth along a child's own percentile line matters more than any single measurement.

## Further reading

For an illustrated growth chart comparison, a sample daily routine for a 4-year-old, and the full list of references, see [How to Grow Taller at 4: A Parent's Guide to Healthy Growth](https://doctortaller.com/blogs/growth-tips/how-to-grow-taller-at-4).

## Disclaimer

This project is for general education and isn't a substitute for advice from a child's pediatrician. Home measurements can vary, so the checker is meant to start a conversation with a doctor, not to diagnose anything.
