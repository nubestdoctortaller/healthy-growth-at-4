What's typical at four

Growth slows after the toddler years. Between ages 3 and 6, most children gain about 2 to 3 inches and 4 to 5 pounds a year, so a preschooler can wear the same clothing size for months at a time.

The CDC growth charts used by U.S. pediatricians show a wide healthy range at age 4:

Genetics sets most of where a child lands in that range. Children of taller parents tend to be taller, and children of shorter parents tend to be shorter.

Everyday habits that support growth

No routine can push a child past their inherited range. These habits help make sure nothing holds growth back.

Sleep
10 to 13 hours in every 24, naps included. A child who has dropped the afternoon nap often needs an earlier bedtime.
Active play
About 3 hours of movement spread through the day, with at least an hour of energetic play. Keep sedentary screen time to an hour or less.
Varied meals
Around 1,000 mg of calcium and 600 IU of vitamin D a day for ages 4 to 8, plus zinc and iron. Extra food or extra vitamins won't add height.
Regular checkups
Well-child visits are where height is measured accurately and plotted over time.

Watch the line, not the dot

A single measurement says little. What matters is whether your child keeps growing along roughly the same percentile line from one checkup to the next. A child who has stayed near the 10th percentile since age 2 is usually growing well. A child who has slipped from the 75th to the 40th percentile over a year is worth a closer look, even though they are still taller.

When to talk to your pediatrician

  • Your child's growth line drifts downward across percentile curves over several visits.
  • Your child measures at or below the 5th percentile.
  • Your child grows noticeably less than 2 to 3 inches over a full year.
  • Anything else about growth or development worries you.

Most short children are healthy. A doctor can confirm it.
